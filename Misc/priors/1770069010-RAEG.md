# Section 2.2 - Absolute Value and the Real Number Line

> [!def] Absolute Value
> The absolute value, of $a \in \mathbb{R}$, is denoted by $|a|$. We define this as:
>
> $$
> 	|a| =
> 	\begin{cases}
> 		a  \quad \\
> 		0 \quad  \\
> 		-a \quad \\
> \end{cases}
> $$

> [!thm] Absolute Value Properties
>
> 1. $\forall ~a,b \in \mathbb{R}, |ab| = |a||b|$
> 2. $\forall ~a \in \mathbb{R}, |a|^2 = a^2$
> 3. If $c \geq 0$, then $|a| \leq  |c|$ iff $-c \leq a \leq c$
> 4. $\forall ~a \in \mathbb{R}, -|a| \leq  a \leq |a|$

> [!pf] Proving Absolute Value Properties
> $$\text{Let } |ab| = |a| |b|$$
> $$\text{If } a=0 \lor b=0 \implies |ab| = 0 \land |a| |b| = 0$$
>
> **_There are four other cases to consider._**
>
> > [!case] (Case 1): If $a>0$ and $b>0$
> >
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > \text{If } & a>0, b>0                    \\
> >            & \implies ab>0               \\
> >            & \implies |ab| = ab = |a||b|
> > \end{align}
> > $$
>
> > [!case] (Case 2): If $a>0$ and $b<0$
> > $$
> > 	\begin{align}
> > 		\text{If } & a>0, b<0                              \\
> > 		           & \implies ab<0                         \\
> > 		           & \implies  |ab| = -ab = a(-b) = |a||b|
> > 	\end{align}
> > $$
>  
> > [!note] Note: The remaining cases are similar to *case 1* and *case 2*
>
> > [!case] (Case 3): If $|a| \leq c$
> > $$
> > 	\begin{align}
> > 		       & a \leq c \land  -a \leq c \\
> > 		\equiv & -c \leq a \leq c
> > 	\end{align}
> > $$
>
> > [!case] (Case 4): Take $c=|a|$ in case 3.

## Triangle Inequality
> [!def] Triangle Inequality 
> If $a,b \in \mathbb{R}$, then $|a+b| \leq |a| + |b|$
> 
> > [!pf] Proof
> > From Case 4, we have: 
> > - $-|a| \leq a \leq |a|$
> > - $-|b| \leq b \leq |b|$
> > Adding these Inequalities we obtain:
> > 
> > $$
> > 	\begin{array}{rcccl}
> > 		 & -|a|       & \leq & a     & \leq & |a|       \\
> > 		+ & -|b|       & \leq & b     & \leq & |b|       \\
> > 		\hline
> > 		  & -|a| - |b| & \leq & a + b & \leq & |a| + |b|
> > 	\end{array}
> > $$
> > 
> > Thus we have: 
> > $$-(|a| + |b|) \leq  a + b \leq  |a| + |b|$$
> > By appling (Case 3) we have:
> > $$|a+b| \leq  |a| + |b|$$
> > 
> > Thus it can be shown that the equality occurs in the triangle inequality: *iff $ab > 0$*
> > $$ |a+b| \leq |a| + |b| \iff ab>0 $$
>
> > [!note] Summary
> > This is to say "the direct path between two points is the shortest"
> 
> > [!cor] Corollary: If $a,b \in \mathbb{R} \implies$
> > - a.) $||a| - |b|| \leq  |a-b|$
> > - b.) $|a-b| \leq  |a| + |b|$
