# Subsequences (Sec-3.4)

Let $X=(x_n)$ be a sequence of real numbers and let: $n_1 < n_2 < ... <$ by a strinctly increasing sequence of natural numbers
Then the sequence $(X_{n_k})_{k=1}$ given by:
$$x_{n_1}, x_{n_2}, ...$$

Then the following:

$$
	\begin{align}
		 & X: \mathbb{N} -> \mathbb{R}        \\
		 & X|_A: A -> \mathbb{R} \text{where}
	\end{align}
$$

Examplle:
$X = \frac{1}{n}$ , then $\frac{1}{2n}$ can be viewed as a subsequence of $X$.
$$ 1/2, ~1/4, ~1/6, ~1/8, ... $$
$$ (\frac{1}{2n}) \text{ can also be viewed as $\frac{1}{2}X$} $$

Theorem:
If $X$ converges to a real number $L$, then any subsequence of $X$ also converges to $L$
^subsequence-convergence

Aside:

$$
	\begin{align}
		\text{Let $X_{n_k}$ be a subsequence of $X$}                                                              \\
		\implies |x_n - L | < \epsilon, \forall ~n \geq n_\epsilon                                                \\
		\text{Our goal now is to satisfy: } |x_{n_k} - L| < \epsilon \forall ~k \geq k_{\epsilon}                 \\
		\text{To do this we must find a } k_{\epsilon} \text{ for which the inequality holds}                     \\
		\text{This is also saying } \exists ~k_{\epsilon} > 0, n_k \geq n_{\epsilon} \forall ~k \geq k_{\epsilon} \\
	\end{align}
$$

$$
	\begin{gather}
		\text{Since } x_n \rightarrow L, \forall ~\epsilon > 0, \exists ~n_{\epsilon} > 0 \ni |x_n - L| < \epsilon, \forall ~n \geq n_{\epsilon} \\
		n_k  \text{ is a monotonic sequence of $\mathbb{N}$} \implies \exists ~k_\epsilon > 0 \ni n_k \geq n_{\epsilon} \forall ~ k \geq k_{\epsilon} \\
		\text{Then } \forall ~ k \geq k_{\epsilon}, |x_{n_k} - x | < \epsilon
	\end{gather}
$$

Consider:
If any subsequence of $X$ converges to $L$, then $x_n \rightarrow L$

Contrapositive:
If $x_n \not \rightarrow L$ then there exists a subsequence of $X$ that does NOT converge to $L$.

## Divergence of a sequence

Theorem:
Let $X$ be a sequence, then the following are equivalent:

1. $X$ does not converge to $L \in \mathbb{R}$
2. $\exists ~ \epsilon_0 > 0 \ni \forall ~k \in \mathbb{N}, \exists ~n_k \in \mathbb{N} \ni n_k \geq k \land |x_{n_k} - L| \geq \epsilon_0$

- Negation of 1.

3. $\exists ~\epsilon_0$ and a subsequence $x_{n_k}$ of $X$ s.t. $|x_{n_k}-L| \geq \epsilon_0, \forall ~k \in \mathbb{N}$

($3 \implies 1$):
Is solved via [the relationship of sequence-subsequence convergence](L09.md#^subsequence-convergence).

($2 \implies 3$):

- $\text{Let } \epsilon_0 \text{ be in } (ii) \text{ for 1, } n_1 \in \mathbb{N} \ni n_1 \geq 1 \land |x_{n_1} - L| \geq \epsilon$
- For $n_1+1 \in \mathbb{N}, \exists ~n_2 \in \mathbb{N}$ s.t. $n_0 \geq n_1+1$ and $|x_{n_2}-L| \geq \epsilon$
- For $n_2+1 \in \mathbb{N}, \exists ~n_3 \in \mathbb{N}$ s.t. $n_0 \geq n_2+1$ and $|x_{n_3}-L| \geq \epsilon$
- ... continues
- For $n_k+1 \in \mathbb{N}, \exists ~n_{k+1} \in \mathbb{N}$ s.t. $n_0 \geq n_k+1$ and $|x_{n_{k+1}}-L| \geq \epsilon$

So $n_k$ is strinctly increasing then $x_{n_k}$ is a subsequence of $X$ and $|x_{n_k}-L| \geq \epsilon_0, \forall ~ n \in \mathbb{N}$

Show that: If $X$ is unbounded, then $\exists ~$ a subsequence $x_{n_k}$ s.t. $\lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{1}{x_{n_k}} = 0$

Proof: 
$$
\begin{gathered}
\frac{1}{x_{n_k}} \rightarrow 0 \\
\frac{1}{x_{n_k}} - 0| < \epsilon \\
\frac{1}{x_{n_k}} < \epsilon \\
\forall ~\epsilon_0, \exists ~k(\epsilon) \ni |\frac{1}{x_{n_k}}| > \frac{1}{\epsilon} , \forall ~k \geq k(\epsilon)
\end{gathered}
$$ 

Recall: Bounded
$$ \exists ~M>0 \ni |x_n| \leq M ~\forall ~n \in \mathbb{N} \implies X \text{ is bounded } $$ 

Recall: Unbounded
$$ \forall ~k \in \mathbb{N}, \exists ~n_k \in \mathbb{N} \ni |x_{n_k}| > k \implies X \text{ is unbounded }$$ 
- Note: if $\forall ~m \geq K, |x_m| \leq k$ then the sequence would be bounded.  $\implies n_k \geq k$ 

Pf: 
$$
\begin{gathered}
\forall ~\epsilon > 0, k=1, \exists ~n_1 \in \mathbb{N} \ni |x_{n_1}| > 1 \\ 
\end{gathered}
$$ 

For $n_1+1 ~\exists ~n_2 \ni |x_{n_2}|>n_2 \land n_2 \geq  n_1+1>n_1$ 
$$ \vdots $$ 
$$n_k+1 ~\exists ~n_2 \ni |x_{n_2}| > n_2 \land n_{k+1} \geq  n_k+1>n_k
$$ 

So we can find a subsequence ($x_{n_k}$) s.t. $|x_{n_k}| > n_{k-1} \forall k \geq 2$

$$ \forall  \epsilon > 0, \exists  M > \frac{1}{\epsilon} \text{ since } n_k \text{ is strictly increaing } \exists k(\epsilon) >0 \ni n_{k-1} > M \forall k \geq k(\epsilon) \implies |x_{n_k}| > \frac{1}{\epsilon} $$

Q: Find a subsequence of $X$ s.t. $|X_{n+k}| > k \forall  ~k \in  \mathbb{N}$
$$ 0 < \frac{1}{X_{n+k}} < \frac{1}{k} $$ 

(Divergence Criteria) $X$ is divergent if:
1. $X$ has two convergent subsequences $X'= (X_{nk})$  and  $X''= (X_{mk})$ whose limits are not equal.
2. $X$ is unbounded 

---

Ex: (pf)
$$0<b<1 \implies \lim_{n \to \infty} b^n=0$$ 

Since ($x_n = b^n$) is decreasing and bounded below by zero, by the [monotone convergence theorem] (x_n) has a limit, denoted by $x$

Note that: ($b^{2n}$) is a subsequences of ($(b^{n})$) then $b^{2n} \rightarrow k$

Note that: $(b^n)^2 = b^{2n} \implies b^{2n} \rightarrow x$

So $x=x^2 \land b^n < b^1 = b<1 \implies x=0$ that is $\lim_{n \to \infty} b^n = 0$
