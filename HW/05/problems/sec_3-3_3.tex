\question{Section 3.3 : 3}{ Let $x_1 \geq 2$ and $x_{n+1} := 1 + \sqrt{x_n - 1}$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Show that $(x_n)$ is decreasing and bounded below by 2. Find the limit. }
\[
	\begin{gathered}
		\text{Observe } 2 \leq  x_1 \text{ Suppose }2 \leq x_k  \\
		{\left\{
		\begin{array}{ccc}
			2=x_{k} & \implies & x_{k+1} = 1+\sqrt{2-1} = 2  = x''              \\
			2<x_{k} & \implies & x_{k+1} = 1+\sqrt{x_k-1} = 1 + \sqrt{x_1} = x'
		\end{array}
		\right .} \implies 
		\left\{
		\begin{array}{ccc}
			2=x_k   & \implies & 2= x_{k+1} \\
			2 < x_k & \implies & 2 < x_{k+1}
		\end{array}
		\right . \\
        \therefore  \forall~ n \in  \mathbb{N}, ~2 \leq x_n 
	\end{gathered}
\]

Therefore we have shown there is a lower bound to the sequence. 

\[
    \begin{gathered}
        \text{Observe: }  2 \leq x_1  \implies 1 \leq x_1 - 1  \implies 1 \leq  \sqrt{x_1 - 1} \implies 2 \leq  1+\sqrt{x_1-1}

        \\
        \text{Suppose }x_{k+1} \leq  x_k \\
        \text{Let }
        \left\{ 
        \begin{array}{ccc}
            x_{k+2} & := & 1+\sqrt{x_{k+1}-1} \\
            x_{k+1} & := & 1+\sqrt{x_{k}-1} \\
        \end{array}
        \right . \\
        x_{k+1} < x_k \implies  \sqrt{x_{k+1}-1} < \sqrt{x_k -1} \implies  x_{k+2} \leq  x_{k+1} \\
        \therefore ~\forall~ n \in  \mathbb{N}, x_n \leq  x_{n-1}
    \end{gathered}
\] 

Therefore we have shown the sequence is decreasing. 

Consider:
\[
    \begin{gathered}
    \lim x_n = x \implies  1+\sqrt{x-1} = 0 \\
    \lim x_n = 1 \lor \lim x_n = 2
    \end{gathered}
\] 

As $x_1$ is defined to be $2 \leq x_1$ the limit must be the bound of the sequence $2$.
