# Limit Theorem (S 3.2)

Recall:

$$
	\begin{align}
		\forall ~\epsilon > 0, ~\exists ~N(\epsilon) > 0 \text{ s.t. } \forall ~n \geq N(\epsilon), |x_n - x| < \epsilon
	\end{align}
$$

> [!def] Bounded Sequence
> A sequence $X$ is bounded if $\exists ~M>0$ s.t. $\forall ~n \in \mathbb{N}, ~|x_n \leq M$
> $$ \text{The set } \{ x_n : n \in \mathbb{N} \} \text{ is a bounded set} $$

> [!thm] A convergent sequence $x_n$ is bounded.
> $$\text{This means the sequences satisifes the condition:}$$
> $$ \forall ~\epsilon > 0, ~\exists ~N(\epsilon) > 0 \text{ s.t. } \forall ~n \geq N(\epsilon), |x_n - x| < \epsilon $$
>
> > [!pf] Example:
> >
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> >     \text{Let } \epsilon=1                                                                  \\
> > ~\exists ~N(\epsilon) > 0~ \text{ s.t. } \forall ~n \geq N(\epsilon), ~|x_n - x| < \epsilon \\
> > \end{align}
> > $$
> >
> > We must find a way to relate this to $|x_n|$
> >
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > \text{So: } |x_n| & = |x_n - x + x|                             \\
> >                   & \leq  |x_n - x | + |x|                      \\
> >                   & < 1 + |x| \forall ~n \geq N(1)              \\
> > M                 & = max \{ x_1, x_2, x_3, ..., |x_{N(1)-1} \}
> > \end{align}
> > $$
>
> This $M$ satisifes as a bound for the tail of the sequence. Where the tail is defined to start at $N(1)$.

    [!thm] a

$$
	\begin{align}
		x_n \rightarrow x
		y_n \rightarrow y
	\end{align}
$$

We have the following operations:

$$
	\begin{align}
		 & \text{Addition}    &  & x_n + y_n     \\
		 & \text{Subtraction} &  & x_n - y_n     \\
		 & \text{Mult}        &  & x_n y_n       \\
		 & \text{Division}    &  & x_n / y_n     \\
		 & \text{Const. Mult} &  & c x_n         \\
		 & \text{Reciprocal}  &  & \frac{1}{y_n}
	\end{align}
$$

[!pf] Addition
$$
x_n + y_n  \rightarrow x+y
$$ 
$$
\begin{align}
    \text{Prove: } \forall ~\epsilon > 0, \exists ~M(\epsilon) \ni  \\
|(x_n + y_n) - (x+y)| < \epsilon \forall ~n \geq N(\epsilon)
\end{align}
$$ 

What do we know?:
$$
\begin{align}
\forall ~\epsilon>0, \exists ~N_x{\epsilon} > 0 \ni  \\
|x_n-x| < \epsilon ~\forall ~ n \geq N_x(\epsilon) \\ \\
\forall ~\epsilon>0, \exists ~N_y{\epsilon} > 0 \ni  \\
|y_n-y| < \epsilon ~\forall ~ n \geq N_y(\epsilon)
\end{align}
$$  

By [Triangle Inequality](1766975367-NRVZ.md)
$$
\begin{align}
|(x_n-x) + (y_n-y)| < \epsilon \\
\leq |(x_n-x)| + |(y_n-y)| < \epsilon \\
< \epsilon/2 + \epsilon/2 \\
\text{If } n \geq N_x(\epsilon) \text{ and } n \geq  N_y(\epsilon) \\
\text{If } n \geq  N_x(\epsilon) + N_y(\epsilon)
\end{align}
$$ 

Bringing it together: 
$$
\begin{align}
    \forall ~ \epsilon>0, ~\exists ~N_x(\epsilon) > 0 \ni \\
\exists ~N_y(\epsilon) > 0 \ni \\
|y_n-y| < \epsilon/2 ~\forall ~n \geq  N_y(\epsilon) \\
\text{Let } N(\epsilon) := N_x(\epsilon) + N_y(\epsilon) \\
\forall ~ n \geq  N(\epsilon), \text{ we have } n \geq N(\epsilon) \land  n \geq N_y(\epsilon) < \epsilon/2 + \epsilon/2 = \epsilon \\
\implies |x_n-x| < \epsilon/2 \land |y_n-y| < \epsilon/2 \\ 
\implies 
\end{align}
$$
