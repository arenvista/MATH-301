A sequence $X=(x_n)$ of real numbers is said to be _bounded_ if there exists a real number $M>0$ s.t. $|X_n|<M \forall ~ n \in \mathbb{N}$

[!?] Prove $\lim_{ n \to \infty} x_ny_n = xy$

[!pf] Backwards Search

$$
	\begin{gather}
		\lim_{ n \to \infty} x_ny_n = xy \\
		|x_ny_n - xy|  = |x_n y_n + x_n y - xy |  \\
		= |x_n(y_n-y) + y(x_n -x) |  \\
		= |x_n(y_n - y)| + |y(x_n-x)| \\
		= |x_n| ~|y_n-y| + |y| ~|x_n-x| < \epsilon \\
		\implies \\
		|x_n| ~|y_n-y| < \frac{\epsilon}{2} ~~~\text{ and }~~~ |y| ~|x_n-x| < \frac{\epsilon}{2} \\
		\iff |x_n-x| < \frac{\epsilon}{2|y|} ~~~\tiny{\text{ if we assume $|y| \neq 0$}} \\
		\tiny{\text{ As $|x_n|$ is convergent there is a bound $M$ } := |x_n| \leq M ~\forall ~n \in \mathbb{N}} \\
		M | y_n - y| < \frac{\epsilon}{2} \iff |y_n -y | < \frac{\epsilon}{2M}
	\end{gather}
$$

Forward Logic:
Case 1: ($y \neq 0$)

$$
	\begin{gather}
		x_n y \text{ is convergent } \implies \text{ it is bounded } \\
		\implies  \exists ~M > 0 \ \ni |x_n| \leq M ~\forall ~n \in \mathbb{N} \\
		\text{Since } y_n \rightarrow y, ~\forall ~\epsilon > 0, \exists ~N_y \in \mathbb{N} \ni \forall ~n \geq N_y, \\
		|y_n -y| < \frac{\epsilon}{2M} \\
		\text{Since } x_n \rightarrow x \land  y \neq 0, \exists ~ N_x \in \mathbb{N} \ni \forall ~ n \geq N_x,  \\
		|x_n -x| < \frac{\epsilon}{2|y|} \\
		\text{Let } V=max \{ N_x, N_y \} ~~\forall ~n \geq N \\
		|x_n y_n - xy| \leq  |x_n |y_n -y | + |y| |x_n -x| \leq  |y_n -y| + |y| |x_n -x| < \epsilon/2 + \epsilon/2 = \epsilon
	\end{gather}
$$

Case 2:

$$
	\begin{gather}
		|x_ny_n - 0 | <  \epsilon \\
		\iff |x_n| ~|y_n| < \epsilon \\
		\iff |x_n| \leq  M\\
		M|y_n| < \epsilon \\
		|y_n -y | < \epsilon/M
	\end{gather}
$$

[!?] Prove: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{y_n} = \frac{1}{y}$

$$
	\begin{gather}
		\frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{y} < \epsilon \\
		\iff \frac{y-y_n}{y_n y} < \epsilon \\
		\frac{1}{|y_n| ~|y|} |y_n - y| < \epsilon \\
		\tiny{\text{Recall: to bound $\frac{a}{b}$ by above we want to increase }} \implies \tiny{\text{ our goal (is this possible?) }}  |y_n| \geq  a  \implies \text{ pick: } ~a = \frac{|y|}{2}\\
		\tiny{\text{Consider subproblem:} \lim_{n \to \infty} y_n = y}
		\epsilon = \frac{|y|}{2}, \exists ~ N_y \in \mathbb{N} \ni \forall ~n \geq N_y, |y_n -y| < \frac{|y|}{2}  \\
		\implies |y_n| \geq \frac{|y|}{2} \\
		|y| = |y-y_n+y_n| \leq  |y_n -y |  + |y_n|  \\
		\implies |y_n| \geq  |y| - |y_n -y | \geq |y| - \frac{|y|}{2} = \frac{|y|}{2} \\
		\implies  |\frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{y}| = \frac{1}{|y_n| ~|y|} |y_n-y| \leq \frac{1}{|y|^2} |y_n-y| < \epsilon \\
		\text{Let } \exists ~\overline{N_y} \ni |y_n -y| < \frac{|y|^2}{2}\epsilon \\
		\text{Since } y_n \rightarrow y, \exists ~ \overline{N_y} \in \mathbb{N} \ni \forall ~n \geq  N_y, |y_n - y | < \frac{|y|^2 }{2}\epsilon \\
		\text{Let } N=max \{ \overline{N_y}, N_y \} ~\forall ~ n \geq N_y \\
		|\frac{1}{y_n}-\frac{1}{y}| \leq  \frac{1}{\frac{|y|^2}{2}} |y_n -y | < \epsilon
	\end{gather}
$$


# Monotonic Sequence (3.3)


[!def] Monotonic Sequence
 Let $X=(x_n)$ be a sequence.
 $X$ is _increasing_ if it satisfies:
 $$ x_1 \leq  x_2 \leq x_3 \leq ... \leq x_n \leq ... $$ 
 $X$ is _decreasing_ if it satisfies:
 $$ x_1 \geq  x_2 \geq x_3 \geq ... \geq x_n \geq ... $$ 
 $X$ is monotonic if it ]is either increasing or decreasing. 


[!thm] Monotonic Convervence Theorem
A monotonic sequence is bounded iff it is bounded.

Case: $X$ is bounded and increasing $\implies \lim_{n \to \infty} = \sup \{ x_n | n \in \mathbb{N} \}$
Case: $X$ is bounded and decreasing $\implies \lim_{n \to \infty} = \inf \{ x_n | n \in \mathbb{N} \}$


[!pf] Proof:  $X$ is bounded and decreasing $\implies \lim_{n \to \infty} = \inf \{ x_n | n \in \mathbb{N} \}$
If $X$ is bounded and decreasing then $-X := (-x_n)$

By Case (a):
$$
\begin{gather}
\lim_{n \to \infty} -x_n = \sup \{ -x_n | n \in \mathbb{N} \}  \\
(-1) \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = (-1) \inf \{ x_n | n \in \mathbb{N} \}  \\
\implies  \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = \inf \{  x_n | n \in \mathbb{N} \}
\end{gather}
$$ 



[!pf] Proof: $X$ is bounded and increasing $\implies \lim_{n \to \infty} = \sup \{ x_n | n \in \mathbb{N} \}$
$$
\begin{gather}
\text{Must prove: } |x_n - x | < \epsilon \iff x-\epsilon < x_n < x+\epsilon  \\ 
\end{gather}
$$ 
By the completeness property $\sup \{  x_n | n \in \mathbb{N} \}$  
we must show $x_n \rightarrow x$

Note that $x_n<x+\epsilon, \forall ~n \in \mathbb{N}$  since $x+\epsilon$ is an upper bound  of $X$

Note that $x-\epsilon$ is not an upper bound of $X$

$$
\implies \exists ~ N \in \mathbb{N} \ni x_N > x-\epsilon \forall ~n \geq N, x_n \geq x_N > x-\epsilon
$$ 

$$
\therefore \forall ~ n \geq N, x-\epsilon<x_n<x+\epsilon \equiv |x_n -x| < \epsilon
$$ 

Let $X=(x_n)$ be defined as $x_1=1$, $x_{n+1} = \sqrt{2 x_n} ~\forall ~n \in \mathbb{N}$. Show $\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n= 2$
Observe: $x_1 = 1, ~x_2 = \sqrt{2}, ~x_3 = \sqrt{2 \sqrt{2}}$
Step 1: Prove by induction $X$ is increasing
$$
\begin{gather}
\text{Case:(n=1) } x_2 \geq  x_1 \\
\text{Suppose: } x_k \leq  x_{k+1} \\
\text{Show: } x_{k+1} \leq x_{k+2} \\
x_{k+1} = \sqrt{2 x_k}, ~~~~x_{k+2} = \sqrt{2 x_{k+1}} \\
\text{... finish inductive statement....}
\end{gather}
$$ 
Step 2: Show $X$ is bounded above by 2. _(induct)_
Case (n=1): x_1 = 1 < 2 
Suppose $x_k \leq 2$
$$
\implies 2x_k \leq 4 \implies \sqrt{2 x_n} \leq \sqrt{4} \implies  x_{k+1} \leq 2
$$ 

$$
\therefore ~x_n \implies  x = \sup X
$$

$$
\begin{gather}
x_{n+1} \rightarrow x \land  \sqrt{2 x_n} \rightarrow \sqrt{2x} \\
x_{n+1} = \sqrt{2x_n} \implies x = \sqrt{2x} \\
x^2 = 2x \implies x(x-2) = 0 \\
\implies \text{roots: } (x=0) \lor (x=2) \\
\text{Observe } x=0 ~\unicode{x21af} \text{ as $X$ is increasing} \\
\therefore ~x = 2
\end{gather}
$$
