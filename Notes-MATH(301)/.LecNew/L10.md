# The Bolzano Weinstrass Theorem

A bounded sequence of $\mathbb{R}$ has a convergent subsequence

## Proof

Since $X_n$ is bounded, $\exists~ I_1 = [a,b] \ni \{ x_n : n \in  \mathbb{N} \} \subseteq I_1$. Let $n_1 = 1$

We now bisect $I_1$ into two equal subintervals: $I_1'$ and $I_1''$.

```tikz

	\draw[latex-latex] (-3.5,0) -- (3.5,0);

\end{document}
```

So one of $I_1'$ and $I_1''$ contains infinitely many elements of $X_n$, denoted by $I_2$

Let $n_2$ be the smallest index of $x_n$ in $I_2$ that is bigger than $n_1$.

We then bisect $I_2$ into two equal intervals $I_2'$ and $I_2''$

One of the $I_2'$ and $I_2''$ contains infinitely many elements of $x_n$ in $I_2$ denoted by $I_3$

Let $n_3$ be the smallest index of $x_n$ in $I_3$ that is bigger than $n_2$.

We continue in this way to obtain a nested sequence of closed and bounded intervals $\{ I_n | n \in  \mathbb{N} \}$ and a strictly increase sequence of natural numbers $\{ n_k | k \in  \mathbb{N} \}$ .

Naturally this means that $X_{n_k}$ is a subsequence of $X_n$.

By the [nested intervals property](Dump/L06%20-%20Intervals.md#intervals#nested-intervals-property), $\exists~ !\xi \in  \cap_{n=1}^\infty I_n$

$$
	\begin{gathered}
		|X_{n_k}-\xi| = |I_k - I_{\xi}|\\
		|X_{n_k}-\xi|  \leq \frac{b-a}{2^{k-1}} \rightarrow 0 \text{ as $k \rightarrow \infty$} \\
		\xi - \frac{b-c}{2^{k-1}} \leq  x_{n_k} \leq \xi  + \frac{b-a}{2^{n-1}}
	\end{gathered}
$$

### Part 1: Explain Like I'm 5 (The "Pigeonhole" Analogy)

Imagine you have an infinite number of pigeons (your sequence), but they are trapped inside a single fenced-in yard (the sequence is bounded).

If you draw a line straight down the middle of the yard, at least one of those two halves **must** contain an infinite number of pigeons. Why? Because if both halves only had a finite number of pigeons, you'd only have a finite number of pigeons in total, which contradicts our setup!

So, you take the half with infinite pigeons, and you cut _that_ in half. Again, one of those smaller sections must contain infinite pigeons. If you keep doing this forever, your fence gets smaller and smaller until it tightly surrounds one exact spot.

Because there are infinite pigeons getting squeezed into this shrinking space, you can easily pick one pigeon from each step to form a line of pigeons (your subsequence) that gets closer and closer to that exact center spot (the limit).

# The Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem

Every bounded sequence in $\mathbb{R}$ has a convergent subsequence.

## Proof

**Step 1: The Initial Bound**

- Let $(x_n)$ be a bounded sequence.
- Because $(x_n)$ is bounded, there exists an initial closed interval $I_1 = [a,b]$ such that the entire sequence is contained within it: $\{x_n : n \in \mathbb{N}\} \subseteq I_1$.
- Let $n_1 = 1$.

**Step 2: The Bisection Method**

- We bisect $I_1$ into two equal subintervals: $I_1'$ and $I_1''$.

```tikz
\usepackage{tikz}
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}
	% Draw the main line with arrows
	\draw[latex-latex] (-3.5,0) -- (3.5,0);
	\draw[red, thick] (0, 0.15) -- (0, -0.15) node[below=5pt] {$\frac{a+b}{2}$};
	% Loop to create ticks and labels
	\foreach \x in {-3,-2,-1,1,2,3}
	\draw (\x, 0.1) -- (\x, -0.1) node[below] {$\x$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}

```

- Because there are infinitely many terms of the sequence but only two subintervals, at least one of $I_1'$ or $I_1''$ must contain infinitely many elements of $x_n$. We denote this chosen subinterval as $I_2$.
- Let $n_2$ be the smallest index of the sequence in $I_2$ such that $n_2 > n_1$.
- We then bisect $I_2$ into two equal subintervals: $I_2'$ and $I_2''$.
- Again, one of these contains infinitely many elements of $x_n$. We denote it as $I_3$.
- Let $n_3$ be the smallest index of the sequence in $I_3$ such that $n_3 > n_2$.

**Step 3: Creating the Subsequence**

- We continue this process infinitely to obtain a nested sequence of closed and bounded intervals $(I_k)_{k=1}^\infty$ and a strictly increasing sequence of natural numbers $(n_k)_{k=1}^\infty$.
- Naturally, this means that $(x_{n_k})$ is a subsequence of $(x_n)$, and $x_{n_k} \in I_k$ for all $k$.

**Step 4: Proving Convergence**

- By the Nested Intervals Property, there exists a unique point $\xi$ that belongs to all intervals: $\exists!\xi \in \bigcap_{k=1}^\infty I_k$.
- Since both our subsequence term $x_{n_k}$ and our limit point $\xi$ are inside the interval $I_k$, the distance between them cannot be greater than the total length of the interval.

$$
	\begin{gathered}
		|x_{n_k} - \xi| \leq \text{length}(I_k) \\
		|x_{n_k} - \xi| \leq \frac{b-a}{2^{k-1}} \rightarrow 0 \text{ as } k \rightarrow \infty \\
		\xi - \frac{b-a}{2^{k-1}} \leq x_{n_k} \leq \xi + \frac{b-a}{2^{k-1}}
	\end{gathered}
$$

Because the length of the interval shrinks to $0$ as $k$ approaches infinity, by the Squeeze Theorem, $x_{n_k}$ converges to $\xi$.

Let $X=(x_n)$ be a bounded sequence.

The _limit superior_ of $x_n$ is the infimum of a set $V$ of $v \in  \mathbb{R}$ s.t. $v < x_n$ for at most a finite number of $n \in  \mathbb{N}$

The _limit inferior_ of $x_n$ is the superma of a set $V$ of $v \in  \mathbb{R}$ s.t. $v < x_n$ for at most a finite number of $n \in  \mathbb{N}$

$$
	\begin{gathered}
		V = \{ v \in  \mathbb{R} : v < x_n \text{ for at most a finite number of } n \in  \mathbb{N} \}  \\
		\iff x_n \leq v  \ \text{ after these indexes} \\
		\forall~ n \in  \mathbb{N}, vx_n \leq v\\
		\implies v \text{ is an upper bound of } v < x_n \text{ for $ n \in  \mathbb{N}_a$ tail of } x_n
	\end{gathered}
$$

$$
	\begin{gathered}
		V = \{ v \in  \mathbb{R} : v < x_n \text{ for at most a finite number of } n \in  \mathbb{N} \}  \\
		\text{ Denoted as: } \overline{\lim} x_n  \equiv \sup \lim x_n \equiv \text{ limit superior}
	\end{gathered}
$$

$$
	\begin{gathered}
		W = \{  w \in  \mathbb{R}: w > x_n \text{ for at most a finite number } n \in  \mathbb{N} \} \\
		\text{ Denoted as: } \underline{\lim} x_n \equiv  \lim \inf x_n
	\end{gathered}
$$

$$ \lim \inf (x_n) = - \lim \sup (-x_n) $$

When the sequence is convergent $\iff \lim \sup = \lim \inf$

$$
	\begin{align}
		 & \text{Consider: } \lim \inf X \leq  \lim \sup X                  \\
		 & \text{If }\lim \inf X = \lim \sup X \implies X \text{ converges} \\
	\end{align}
$$

Intuition:

$$
	\begin{gathered}
		\text{Consider: } X = (-1)^n \\
		V = [1, \infty)  \quad W = (-\infty, -1]\\
	\end{gathered}
$$

Proof:

$$
	\begin{gathered}
		\text{Let } x_n \text{ be convergent, } \lim_{n \to \infty} X = L \\
		\forall~  \epsilon > 0, \exists~ n_\epsilon > 0 \ni  \\
		|x_n - L | < \epsilon ~\forall~ n \geq n_\epsilon \\
		\text{That is } \forall~ n \geq n_\epsilon,  L-\epsilon < x_n < L + \epsilon  \\
		\implies  L+\epsilon \in  V \text{ and } L-\epsilon \in  W \\
		\implies \lim \sup x_n \leq  L + \epsilon \text{ and } \lim \inf x_n \geq L-\epsilon \\
\implies \forall~ \epsilon>0, ~~L-\epsilon \leq \lim \inf x_n \leq \lim \sup x_n \leq \lim \sup x_n \leq  x + \epsilon \\
\implies \lim \inf x_n = \lim \sup x_n
	\end{gathered}
$$
