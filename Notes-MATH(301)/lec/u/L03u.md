# Ordering Properties of $\mathbb{R}$

## Positive Sets and Order

> [!def] **Set of Positive Real Numbers**
> A nonempty subset $P \subset \mathbb{R}$ is called the **set of positive real numbers** if:
>
> 1. If $a,b \in P$, then $a+b \in P$.
> 2. If $a,b \in P$, then $ab \in P$.
> 3. (**Trichotomy Law**) For every $a \in \mathbb{R}$ exactly one holds:
>
>    * $a \in P$
>    * $a = 0$
>    * $-a \in P$

---

### Order Notation

> [!def] **Order Relation**
>
> * $a>b$ if $a-b \in P$
> * $a \ge b$ if $a-b \in P \cup {0}$
>
> For any $a,b \in \mathbb{R}$ exactly one of the following holds:
>
> * $a>b$
> * $a<b$
> * $a=b$

---

# Fundamental Order Properties

> [!thm] **Transitivity of $>$**
> If $a>b$ and $b>c$, then $a>c$.

> [!pf]
> $a>b \Rightarrow a-b \in P$
> $b>c \Rightarrow b-c \in P$
>
> Since
> $$
> a-c = (a-b)+(b-c),
> $$
> and $P$ is closed under addition, we have $a-c \in P$.
> Hence $a>c$. ∎

---

> [!thm] **Translation Property**
> If $a>b$, then $a+c>b+c$ for all $c\in\mathbb{R}$.

> [!pf]
> $a>b \iff a-b\in P$.
>
> Observe
> $$
> (a+c)-(b+c)=a-b.
> $$
> Thus $(a+c)-(b+c)\in P$, so $a+c>b+c$. ∎

---

> [!thm] **Multiplication and Inequalities**
>
> 1. If $a>b$ and $c>0$, then $ac>bc$.
> 2. If $a>b$ and $c<0$, then $ac<bc$.

> [!pf]
> If $a>b$, then $a-b\in P$.
>
> If $c>0$, then $c\in P$.
> Since $P$ is closed under multiplication,
> $$
> (a-b)c \in P.
> $$
> Hence $ac-bc \in P$, so $ac>bc$.
>
> If $c<0$, then $-c>0$ and applying the first result to $(a-b)(-c)$ reverses the inequality. ∎

---

> [!thm] **Square of a Nonzero Number is Positive**
> If $a\neq0$, then $a^2>0$.

> [!pf]
> By trichotomy, either $a\in P$ or $-a\in P$.
>
> > [!case] $a\in P$
> > Then $a^2=aa\in P$.
>
> > [!case] $-a\in P$
> > Then $(-a)^2\in P$, but $(-a)^2=a^2$.
>
> Hence $a^2>0$. ∎

---

> [!thm] **$1>0$**

> [!pf]
> First note $1\neq0$. Otherwise, from $a\cdot1=a$ we would obtain $a=0$ for all $a\in\mathbb{R}$, contradiction.
>
> Since $1\neq0$, the previous theorem gives $1^2>0$.
> Hence $1>0$. ∎

---

> [!thm] **Natural Numbers are Positive**
> For all $n\in\mathbb{N}$, $n>0$.

> [!pf]
> Base case: $1>0$.
>
> Assume $k>0$. Then $k\in P$ and $1\in P$.
> Since $P$ is closed under addition, $k+1\in P$.
>
> Thus by induction all natural numbers are positive. ∎

---

> [!thm] **Sign of a Product**
>
> 1. If $ab>0$, then either both are positive or both are negative.
> 2. If $ab<0$, then exactly one is positive.

> [!pf]
> Use trichotomy and the multiplication property of $P$.
>
> If $ab>0$, then $ab\in P$.
> This can only occur if:
>
> * $a,b\in P$, or
> * $-a,-b\in P$.
>
> The second statement follows similarly. ∎

# Absolute Value

## Definition

> [!def] **Absolute Value**
> For $a\in\mathbb{R}$,
> $$
> |a|=
> \begin{cases}
> a & a>0 \
> 0 & a=0 \
> -a & a<0
> \end{cases}
> $$

---

## Basic Properties

> [!thm] **Multiplicative Property**
> $|ab|=|a||b|$.

> [!pf]
> Follows directly from case analysis on signs of $a$ and $b$. ∎

---

> [!thm] **Square Property**
> $|a|^2=a^2$.

> [!pf]
> $$
> |a|^2=|a||a|=|a^2|=a^2.
> $$ ∎

---

> [!thm] **Absolute Value Inequality**
> If $c\ge0$, then
> $$
> |a|\le c \iff -c\le a\le c.
> $$

> [!pf]
> Directly from the definition of $|a|$ by cases. ∎

---

> [!cor] **Bounding by Absolute Value**
> $$
> -|a|\le a\le |a|.
> $$

---

## Triangle Inequality

> [!thm] **Triangle Inequality**
> $$
> |a+b|\le |a|+|b|.
> $$

> [!pf]
> Since
> $$
> -|a|\le a\le |a|,\quad -|b|\le b\le |b|,
> $$
> adding yields
> $$
> -|a|-|b|\le a+b\le |a|+|b|.
> $$
> Hence $|a+b|\le |a|+|b|$. ∎

---

> [!cor] **Reverse Triangle Inequality**
> $$
> ||a|-|b||\le |a-b|.
> $$

> [!pf]
> Apply the triangle inequality to $a=(a-b)+b$ and rearrange. ∎

# Section 2.3 — The Completeness Property of $\mathbb{R}$

## Bounds

> [!def] **Upper and Lower Bounds**
>
> Let $S\subset\mathbb{R}$, $S\neq\emptyset$.
>
> * $S$ is **bounded above** if $\exists u$ such that $s\le u$ for all $s\in S$.
>   Such $u$ is an **upper bound**.
>
> * $S$ is **bounded below** if $\exists w$ such that $s\ge w$ for all $s\in S$.
>   Such $w$ is a **lower bound**.
>
> $S$ is **bounded** if it is bounded above and below.

---

## Supremum and Infimum

> [!def] **Supremum**
> If $S$ is bounded above, a number $u$ is the **supremum** (least upper bound) if:
>
> 1. $u$ is an upper bound of $S$.
> 2. If $v$ is any upper bound, then $u\le v$.
>
> Notation: $\sup S$.

---

> [!def] **Infimum**
> If $S$ is bounded below, a number $w$ is the **infimum** (greatest lower bound) if:
>
> 1. $w$ is a lower bound of $S$.
> 2. If $t$ is any lower bound, then $t\le w$.
>
> Notation: $\inf S$.

---

> [!lem] **Characterization of Supremum**
> A number $u$ is $\sup S$ iff:
>
> 1. $s\le u$ for all $s\in S$, and
> 2. For every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $s_\varepsilon\in S$ such that
>    $$
>    s_\varepsilon>u-\varepsilon.
>    $$
