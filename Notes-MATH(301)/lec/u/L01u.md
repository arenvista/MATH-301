# Section 1.3 — Finite and Infinite Sets

## Basic Definitions

> [!def] **Empty Set**
> The **empty set**, denoted $\emptyset$, is the set containing no elements.

---

> [!def] **Finite Set / Cardinality $n$**
> A set $S$ has **$n$ elements** (where $n \in \mathbb{N}$) if there exists a bijection
> $$
> f : {1,2,\dots,n} \to S.
> $$
> In this case we write $|S| = n$.

---

> [!def] **Finite and Infinite Sets**
> A set $S$ is **finite** if either:
>
> * $S = \emptyset$, or
> * there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $|S| = n$.
>
> A set is **infinite** if it is not finite.

---

## Main Structural Result

> [!thm] **Subset Principle for Finite and Infinite Sets**
> Let $T \subset S$.
>
> 1. If $S$ is finite, then $T$ is finite.
> 2. If $T$ is infinite, then $S$ is infinite.

---

### Proof

> [!pf]
> We prove (1) by induction on the number of elements of $S$.
>
> **Base Case:**
> Suppose $|S| = 1$. Then $S = {a}$.
> Any subset $T \subset S$ is either $\emptyset$ or ${a}$.
> In either case, $T$ is finite.
>
> **Inductive Hypothesis:**
> Assume that every subset of a set with $k$ elements is finite.
>
> **Inductive Step:**
> Let $|S| = k+1$, and suppose
> $$
> f : {1,2,\dots,k+1} \to S
> $$
> is a bijection.
>
> Let $T \subset S$.
>
> ---
>
> > [!case] **Case 1: $f(k+1) \notin T$**
> > Then
> > $$
> > T \subset S \setminus {f(k+1)}.
> > $$
> > The set $S \setminus {f(k+1)}$ has $k$ elements.
> > By the inductive hypothesis, $T$ is finite.
>
> ---
>
> > [!case] **Case 2: $f(k+1) \in T$**
> > Then
> > $$
> > T \setminus {f(k+1)} \subset S \setminus {f(k+1)}.
> > $$
> > The latter set has $k$ elements, so by induction
> > $T \setminus {f(k+1)}$ is finite.
> >
> > Therefore there exists a bijection
> > $$
> > g : {1,2,\dots,m} \to T \setminus {f(k+1)}.
> > $$
> >
> > Define a function
> > $$
> > g_a : {1,2,\dots,m,m+1} \to T
> > $$
> > by
> > $$
> > g_a(i)=
> > \begin{cases}
> > g(i), & 1 \le i \le m, \
> > f(k+1), & i=m+1.
> > \end{cases}
> > $$
> >
> > This function is a bijection, so $T$ has $m+1$ elements and is finite.
>
> ---
>
> This completes the inductive proof of (1).
>
> **Part (2)** follows immediately from (1):
> If $S$ were finite, then every subset $T \subset S$ would be finite.
> Therefore, if $T$ is infinite, $S$ must also be infinite.
> ∎

---

## Logical Structure Summary

> [!imp] **Key Consequences**
>
> * Subsets of finite sets are finite.
> * Supersets of infinite sets are infinite.
> * Finiteness is preserved downward under inclusion.
> * Infiniteness is preserved upward under inclusion.
