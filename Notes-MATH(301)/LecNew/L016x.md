### **Problem Statement**
Determine a $\delta$ for $|x - 4| < \delta$ that guarantees:
$$
|\sqrt{x} - 2| < \frac{1}{2}
$$

---

### **Method 1: Direct Algebraic Manipulation**
This method works backward from the $\epsilon$ inequality to find the exact boundaries for $x$, and then determines the maximum allowable distance from $4$.

**Step 1: Isolate $x$ in the inequality**
$$
\begin{gathered}
|\sqrt{x} - 2| < \frac{1}{2} \\
-\frac{1}{2} < \sqrt{x} - 2 < \frac{1}{2} \\
\frac{3}{2} < \sqrt{x} < \frac{5}{2}
\end{gathered}
$$

**Step 2: Square all parts to remove the square root**
*(Since all terms are positive, the inequality is preserved)*
$$
\frac{9}{4} < x < \frac{25}{4}
$$

**Step 3: Subtract 4 (which is $\frac{16}{4}$) to build $|x - 4|$**
$$
\begin{gathered}
\frac{9}{4} - \frac{16}{4} < x - 4 < \frac{25}{4} - \frac{16}{4} \\
-\frac{7}{4} < x - 4 < \frac{9}{4}
\end{gathered}
$$

**Step 4: Choose $\delta$**
To guarantee that $x - 4$ stays within this asymmetric interval, we must choose the *smaller* distance to the endpoints. The distance to the left endpoint is $7/4$, and the distance to the right endpoint is $9/4$. 
$$
\delta = \frac{7}{4}
$$
This guarantees that $-\frac{7}{4} < x - 4 < \frac{7}{4}$, which safely satisfies the original condition.

---

### **Method 2: Bounding a Factor (The Conjugate Method)**
This method uses algebraic manipulation to factor out $|x - 4|$ and establishes an upper bound ($D$) for the remaining part of the expression.

**Step 1: Multiply by the conjugate**
$$
|\sqrt{x} - 2| = \left| \frac{(\sqrt{x} - 2)(\sqrt{x} + 2)}{\sqrt{x} + 2} \right| = \frac{|x - 4|}{\sqrt{x} + 2}
$$

**Step 2: Restrict $\delta$ to bound the denominator**
Let's arbitrarily restrict our distance from $4$ by assuming $|x - 4| < 2$ (this is our preliminary $c = 2$). 
$$
\begin{gathered}
-2 < x - 4 < 2 \\
2 < x < 6
\end{gathered}
$$
Since $x > 2$, we know that $\sqrt{x} > \sqrt{2}$. Therefore, $\sqrt{x} + 2 > \sqrt{2} + 2$. 
This gives us a maximum bound ($D$) for our fraction:
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2} < \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} + 2}
$$

**Step 3: Set up the final inequality**
Substitute this bound back into our conjugate expression and set it less than our $\epsilon$ value ($\frac{1}{2}$):
$$
\begin{gathered}
|\sqrt{x} - 2| < \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} + 2} |x - 4| < \frac{1}{2} \\
\end{gathered}
$$

**Step 4: Solve for $|x - 4|$**
Multiply both sides by $\sqrt{2} + 2$:
$$
|x - 4| < \frac{\sqrt{2} + 2}{2}
$$

**Step 5: Define the final $\delta$**
Because we made an initial assumption that $|x - 4| < 2$, our final $\delta$ must be the minimum of our initial restriction ($c=2$) and our calculated value:
$$
\delta = \min \left\{ 2, \frac{\sqrt{2} + 2}{2} \right\}
$$
Since $\frac{\sqrt{2} + 2}{2} \approx 1.707$ (which is less than 2), our final answer is:
$$
\delta = \frac{\sqrt{2} + 2}{2} \approx 1.707
$$

*(Note: Both Method 1 and Method 2 yield valid $\delta$ values. Method 1 gives an exact bound of $1.75$, while Method 2 gives a slightly more conservative, but perfectly mathematically valid, bound of $1.707$.)*

---
### **Preliminary Analysis (Why we use this method)**
* **Monotone Convergence Theorem?** Cannot be used. The given condition bounds the *distance* between terms, but it does not dictate their *direction*. The sequence could be oscillating.
* **Contractive Sequence Theorem?** Cannot be directly cited. A contractive sequence requires $|x_{n+2} - x_{n+1}| \leq C |x_{n+1} - x_n|$ for some constant $C < 1$. We are given an absolute bound ($r^n$), not a relative one.
* **Conclusion:** We must prove the sequence is Cauchy directly using the definition. Since every Cauchy sequence of real numbers is convergent, proving it is Cauchy proves it converges.

---

### **Proof: The Sequence is Cauchy**

**Given:** A sequence $(x_n)$ where $0 < r < 1$, and for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $|x_{n+1} - x_n| < r^n$.

**To Prove:** For any $\epsilon > 0$, there exists an $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $m, n \geq N$, $|x_m - x_n| < \epsilon$.

**Step 1: Set up the distance between $x_m$ and $x_n$**
Without loss of generality, assume $m > n$. We can express the difference $x_m - x_n$ as a telescoping sum by adding and subtracting all the intermediate terms:
$$
|x_m - x_n| = |(x_m - x_{m-1}) + (x_{m-1} - x_{m-2}) + \dots + (x_{n+1} - x_n)|
$$

**Step 2: Apply the Triangle Inequality and our Given Condition**
Using the triangle inequality ($|A + B| \leq |A| + |B|$), we can split this into a sum of absolute values:
$$
|x_m - x_n| \leq |x_m - x_{m-1}| + |x_{m-1} - x_{m-2}| + \dots + |x_{n+1} - x_n|
$$
Now, substitute the given condition $|x_{k+1} - x_k| < r^k$ for each term:
$$
|x_m - x_n| < r^{m-1} + r^{m-2} + \dots + r^n
$$

**Step 3: Factor out $r^n$ and apply the Geometric Series formula**
Factor the lowest power, $r^n$, out of the polynomial:
$$
|x_m - x_n| < r^n(1 + r + r^2 + \dots + r^{m-n-1})
$$
The expression in the parentheses is a finite geometric series. As you noted in your scratchpad, the sum of a finite geometric series $S = 1 + r + r^2 + \dots + r^k$ is $\frac{1 - r^{k+1}}{1 - r}$. Applying this to our series:
$$
|x_m - x_n| < r^n \left( \frac{1 - r^{m-n}}{1 - r} \right)
$$

**Step 4: Establish a simpler, absolute upper bound**
Because $0 < r < 1$ and $m > n$, we know that $r^{m-n}$ is a positive number. Therefore, $1 - r^{m-n} < 1$. 
This allows us to simplify our inequality by replacing the numerator with 1, making the right side even larger:
$$
|x_m - x_n| < \frac{r^n}{1 - r}
$$

**Step 5: The formal $\epsilon$-$N$ conclusion**
Let $\epsilon > 0$ be given. We need to force our upper bound to be less than $\epsilon$:
$$
\frac{r^n}{1 - r} < \epsilon \implies r^n < \epsilon(1 - r)
$$
Because $0 < r < 1$, we know that $\lim_{n \to \infty} r^n = 0$. 
By the definition of a limit, since $\epsilon(1 - r)$ is a fixed positive number, there must exist some integer $N$ such that for all $n \geq N$:
$$
r^n < \epsilon(1 - r)
$$
Therefore, for all $m > n \geq N$, it follows that:
$$
|x_m - x_n| < \frac{r^n}{1 - r} < \epsilon
$$
Thus, the sequence $(x_n)$ is Cauchy. Because it is a Cauchy sequence of real numbers, it is convergent. $\blacksquare$
