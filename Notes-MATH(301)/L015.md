# Sequential Criterion for Limits

Let $f: A \to \mathbb{R}$ and let $c$ be a cluster point of $A$. The following are equivalent:

1. $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$
2. For every sequence $(x_n)$ in $A$ with $x_n \neq c$ and $x_n \to c$, we have $f(x_n) \to L$

---

## Proof

### (1) $\implies$ (2):
Assume $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$. Let $(x_n)$ be a sequence in $A$ with $x_n \neq c$ and $x_n \to c$. We want to show $f(x_n) \to L$.

By the definition of the limit, for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $\delta = \delta(\epsilon) > 0$ such that for all $x \in A$ with $0 < |x-c| < \delta$, we have $|f(x) - L| < \epsilon$.

Since $x_n \to c$ and $x_n \neq c$, there exists $N$ such that for all $n \geq N$, $0 < |x_n - c| < \delta$. Thus, for $n \geq N$, $|f(x_n) - L| < \epsilon$. Therefore, $f(x_n) \to L$.

### (2) $\implies$ (1):
Assume for every sequence $x_n$ in $A$ with $x_n \neq c$ and $x_n \to c$, we have $f(x_n) \to L$. Suppose, for contradiction, that $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) \neq L$. Then, there exists $\epsilon_0 > 0$ such that for all $\delta > 0$, there exists $x \in A$ with $0 < |x - c| < \delta$ and $|f(x) - L| \geq \epsilon_0$.

For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, take $\delta = 1/n$. Then there exists $x_n \in A$ with $0 < |x_n - c| < 1/n$ and $|f(x_n) - L| \geq \epsilon_0$. Then $x_n \to c$, $x_n \neq c$ for all $n$, but $|f(x_n) - L| \geq \epsilon_0$ for all $n$, so $f(x_n)$ does not converge to $L$. This contradicts our assumption. Thus, $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$.

---

**Conclusion:** $(1) \iff (2)$.

---

Example: 
$$
    \lim_{x \to 0} sin(\frac{1}{x}) ~\text{DNE} \in \mathbb{R}
$$ 

Proof: 

Choose: $x_n = \frac{1}{2n \pi}$

$x_n \rightarrow 0$ as $n \rightarrow \infty$ and $x_n \neq 0, \forall~ n \in \mathbb{N}$

Thus $sin(\frac{1}{x_n}) = sin(2n \pi) = 0$


Choose $y_n = \frac{1}{2n \pi + \frac{\pi}{2}}$

$y_n \rightarrow  0$ as $n \rightarrow  \infty$ and $y_n \neq 0, \forall~ n \in \mathbb{N}$ 

So: $(\frac{1}{y_n}) = sin(2n \pi + \frac{pi}{2}) = sin (\frac{pi}{2}) = 1$

## Applications in Limit Theorems


$$
\begin{gathered}
\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L 
\quad \quad
\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = M \\
\lim_{x \to c} \left( f(x) + g(x) \right) = L + M \\
\lim_{x \to c} f(g(x)) = LM \\
\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L}{M} \\
\lim_{x \to c} c \cdot f(x) = cL
\end{gathered}
$$ 


Proof: $\lim_{x \to c} \left( f(x) + g(x) \right) = L + M \\$

For every sequence $(x_n) \in A$ with $x_n \rightarrow c$ and $x_n \not\in c$, $\forall~  n \in \mathbb{N}$. 

BWS:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\lim_{x \to c} \left( f(x) + g(x) \right) = L + M  \\
\leftarrow \begin{cases}
f(x_n) \rightarrow L \\
g(x_n) \rightarrow M
\end{cases}
\end{gathered}
$$

Since $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ by $(1) \implies (2)$, $f(x_n) \rightarrow L$

Since $\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = L$ by $(1) \implies (2)$, $g(x_n) \rightarrow M$

By limit theorem of sequences: $f(x_n) + g(x_n) \rightarrow L+M$

By $(2) \implies (1)$ we can say $\lim_{x \to c} f(x_n) + g(x_n) \rightarrow L+M$

## Squeeze Theorem for Functions

$$
\begin{gathered}
\forall~ x \in A ~\land~ x \neq c \quad \quad f(x) \leq g(x) \leq h(x) \\
\\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L ~\land~ \lim_{x \to c} h(x) = L \implies \lim_{x \to c} g(x)  \\
\end{gathered}
$$ 

### Proof:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\lim_{x \to c} f(x) =  L \overset{(1) \implies (2)}{\implies} f(x_n) \rightarrow L \\
\lim_{x \to c} h(x) =  L \overset{(1) \implies (2)}{\implies} h(x_n) \rightarrow L \\
\text{Observe } \forall~ n \in \mathbb{N}, ~ f(x_n) \leq  g(x_n) \leq h(x_n)
\end{gathered}
$$ 
By the sequeeze theorem on sequences: 
$$ \lim_{x \to c} g(x_n) = L \implies \lim_{x \to c} g(x) = L $$
